Reducing the variance in KL estimator

August 20, 2026 · Muyu He

Problem. The standard estimator (”k1”) of the KL divergence at each token position between target distribution \(p\) and reference distribution \(q\), ie. \(\mathbb{E}_{x \sim p}[\log (p(x)/q(x))]\) is estimated with \(\log (p(x)/q(x))\) sampling the actual token \(x\). But the variance of this estimator based over sampled tokens \(x\) is high, which lead to training instability.

Contributor of variance in the k1 estimator

Let \(r = p(x)/q(x), \delta = r - 1\). Assuming \(|\delta | \lt 1\) (ie. \(p/q \lt 2\)) for the current token \(x\), we can rewrite \(\log r\) as:

$$ \begin{aligned} \log r &= \log (1+ \delta) \\ &= \log (1) + \int_{0}^{\delta} \frac{\partial \log (1+ t)}{\partial t} dt \\ &= \log (1) + \int_{0}^{\delta} \frac{1}{1+t} dt \\ &= \log (1) + \int_{0}^{\delta} (1- t + t^2 - t^3 + ...) dt \ \ \text{ if } |\delta| \lt 1 \\ &= \log (1) + \left[t- \frac{t^2}{2} + \frac{t^3}{3} - \frac{t^4}{4} + ...\right]_{0}^{\delta} \\ &= \left[t- \frac{t^2}{2} + \frac{t^3}{3} - \frac{t^4}{4} + ...\right]_{0}^{\delta} \\ &= \delta - \frac{\delta^2}{2} + \frac{\delta^3}{3} - \frac{\delta^4}{4} + ... \end{aligned} $$

The derivation uses the fact that the geometric series of \(1/(1+t)\) is \(1- t + t^2 - t^3 + ...\)..

Add a 0-expectation term \((1/r-1)\)

The term \((1/r-1)\) has an expectation over tokens of 0. Therefore, the term can be added to the k1 estimator \(\log r\) without changing the expectation, forming another unbiased estimator (”k3”):

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p} [\frac{1}{r}-1] &= \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p} [\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}-1] \\ &= \sum_{x}p(x) (\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}-1) \\ &= \sum_{x}(q(x) - p(x)) \\ &= \sum_{x}q(x) - \sum_{x}p(x) \\ &= 1 - 1 = 0 \\ \therefore \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p}[\log r] &= \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p}[\log r + \frac{1}{r} - 1] \end{aligned} $$

Geometric series of the 0-expectation term

If we expand \(1/r\) around 1, given \(|\delta | \lt 1\), we get:

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{1}{r} &= \frac{1}{1+\delta} = 1 - \delta + \delta^2 - \delta^3 + ... \\ \therefore \frac{1}{r} -1 &= - \delta + \delta^2 - \delta^3 + ... \end{aligned} $$

Cancelling the linear term in k3

Assuming each token in the vocabulary satisfies \(|\delta|\lt 1\), we can expand the geometric series inside the expectation of new k3 estimator. As a result, we get the following formula which cancels the linear term:

$$ \begin{aligned} (\log r) + (\frac{1}{r}) - 1 &= (\delta - \frac{\delta^2}{2} + \frac{\delta^3}{3} - \frac{\delta^4}{4} + ...) + (- \delta + \delta^2 - \delta^3 + ...) \\ &= \frac{\delta^2}{2} - \frac{2 \delta^3}{3} + \frac{3 \delta^4}{4} - \frac{4 \delta^5}{5} + ... \end{aligned} $$

Condition for the formula. Since the geometric series expansion is only possible if each vocabulary token in this position satisfies \(|\delta|\lt 1\), which is intuitively expected, it will not be expanded if some tokens do not satisfy this condition, ie. \(p/q \gt 2\). In that case, the k3 estimator will still be unbiased, because of an addition of 0-expectation term. However, the k3 estimator cannot be rewritten in a way that removes an materially contributing term.

K3 estimator is non-negative

We can prove that k3 is nonnegative by first finding its global minimum given \(\delta\):

$$ \begin{gathered} \begin{aligned} \text{Let } f(\delta) &= \log (1+\delta) + (\frac{1}{1+\delta}) - 1 \\ \therefore f'(\delta) &= \frac{1}{1+\delta} - \frac{1}{(1+\delta)^2} \\ &= \frac{\delta}{(1+\delta)^2} \end{aligned} \\[0.8em] \because f'(\delta) = \frac{\delta}{(1+\delta)^2} = \begin{cases} \lt 0 & -1 \lt \delta \lt 0 \\ 0 & \delta = 0 \\ \gt 0 & \delta \gt 0 \end{cases} \\[0.8em] \therefore \delta \text{ is the global minimizer} \\ \therefore \text{min}f(\delta) = 0 \end{gathered} $$

K3 estimator is likely to have lower variance than k1 at a token position when \(|\delta|\ll 1\), but not necessarily otherwise

After expansion, as \(\epsilon \rightarrow 0\) (ie. \(|\delta|\ll1\)), k1 value is \(O(\epsilon)\) and k3 value is \(O(\epsilon^2)\). So k1’s variance is \(O(\epsilon^2)\) and k3’s variance is \(O(\epsilon^4)\). 1. If \(\epsilon \rightarrow 0\)for a sufficient probability mass of vocabulary tokens, k3’s total variance will generally be dominated by the \(\delta^4\) term, making it smaller than k1’s dominating \(\delta^2\) term. However, if \(|\delta|\) is large, the domination argument will not work. Instead, we can only write the analytic solution of \(\text{Var}(k3)-\text{Var}(k1)\) that depends on \(p,q\):

$$ \begin{aligned} \text{Let } C &= \frac{1}{r} -1 = \frac{q}{p}-1 \\ \therefore k3 &= k1 + C \\ \because \text{Var}(k3)-\text{Var}(k1) &= \text{Var}(k1+C)-\text{Var}(k1) \\ &= \text{Var}(C) + 2 \text{Cov}(k1, C) \\ &= \sum_i^{\text{vocab}} p_i \left( C - E[C] \right)^2 + 2 \sum_i^{\text{vocab}} p_i \left( \log r \cdot C \right) \\ &= \sum_i^{\text{vocab}} p_i \left( C - 0 \right)^2 + 2 \sum_i^{\text{vocab}} p_i \left( \log r \cdot C \right) \\ &= \sum_i^{\text{vocab}} p_i \left( C^2 + 2 \log r \cdot C \right) \\ &= \sum_i^{\text{vocab}} p_i \left( (\frac{1}{r}-1)^2 + 2 \log r \cdot (\frac{1}{r}-1) \right) \\ &= \sum_i^{\text{vocab}} p_i \left( (\frac{1}{r}-1)^2 - 2 \log \frac{1}{r} \cdot (\frac{1}{r}-1) \right) \end{aligned} $$

When is \(\text{Var}(k3)\gt\text{Var}(k1)\). When \(q\) is significantly larger than \(p\) at a sufficient probability mass of vocabulary tokens, we will have \(1/r \gg 1\), meaning the quadratic term \((1/r-1)^2\) dominates in total variance. Therefore, \(\text{Var}(k3)-\text{Var}(k1)\) comes out as positive, making k3 having higher variance among sampled tokens.
